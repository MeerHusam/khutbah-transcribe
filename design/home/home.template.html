Why I made this

Every Friday, many non-Arabic speakers around me in the masjid couldn't understand the khutbah. The khutbah is for all of us, so I wanted everyone in the room to be able to understand it in full, including the ayaat and hadith the imam builds it on.

For now each khutbah is recorded, translated and put here after Jumu'ah. The next step, and the more important one, is following it in your language while the imam is speaking.

I built Khutbah.dev and look after it. Every khutbah here is one I've processed, reviewed, and chosen to publish. I'm also a hafidh of the Qur'an, alhamdulillah, which is a big part of why the Quranic references matter to me: when the imam recites an ayah, I want every listener to know exactly which ayah it is and what it means.

The technical details

Arabic first

Audio is transcribed using Gemini 3.5 Flash, which handles dense Arabic speech well. Groq Whisper runs in parallel for word-level timestamps, over the whole file and again in short overlapping windows so no stretch of the audio is skipped. The outputs are aligned using Needleman-Wunsch sequence matching, so the final transcript carries Gemini's text with Whisper's real audio timing.

Read as it was said

The transcript is split at breath-pause boundaries rather than fixed word counts. Claude (Sonnet 5.5) translates each segment with the full khutbah in view, so the English reads as the imam intended rather than as a word-for-word substitution. Where the imam quotes an ayah or a hadith mid-sentence, the published translation (Sahih International for ayaat, sunnah.com for hadith) replaces Claude's wording.

Nothing missed

Detection runs in three passes: Claude catches explicit citations ("قال الله تعالى"), a sliding-window scan finds ayahs cited without a signal phrase, and a 4-gram index catches partial recitations the other two miss. Each ayah is matched against the corpus and linked to quran.com. The full ayah is shown with the recited part in bold, alongside the Sahih International translation.

Linked to the source

Claude flags the hadith the imam quotes. The Arabic matn is searched on sunnah.com, preferring the collection the imam names ("رواه مسلم"), and a result is only accepted if its wording matches what was said. The link and narrator come from that sunnah.com page, so they are right regardless of numbering differences between hadith databases.
